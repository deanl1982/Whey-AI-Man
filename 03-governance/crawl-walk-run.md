## Overview

The **Crawl, Walk, Run** framework is Microsoft's maturity model for managing organisational change and adoption of new technologies, including generative AI. This framework provides a structured, iterative approach to introducing transformative technologies into a production enterprise environment while managing risk, building capability, and ensuring measurable value delivery.

While the framework originates from Microsoft's FinOps practices, it has been adapted across various technology adoption scenarios, including cloud migration, security modernization, and—most relevantly—generative AI integration into enterprise environments.

## The Three Phases

### 1. **Crawl** - Foundation and Experimentation Phase

**Objective:** Build awareness, establish foundational knowledge, and validate use cases with minimal risk.

#### Key Characteristics:
- **Scope:** Limited pilots with specific departments or use cases
- **Risk Level:** Low (limited exposure, controlled environments)
- **Investment:** Focused, resource-efficient pilots
- **Governance:** Basic controls, manual processes
- **Metrics:** Learning and proof-of-concept validation

#### Activities in the Crawl Phase:
- **Identify champions and advocates** - Find internal stakeholders who understand generative AI and can champion adoption
- **Establish baseline understanding** - Conduct training and awareness programs across leadership and technical teams
- **Start with high-value, low-complexity use cases** - Choose 1-3 initial applications where ROI is clear
- **Build a pilot program** - Run controlled experiments with specific teams
- **Set up initial governance structures** - Establish basic policies around data security, acceptable use, and ethical AI use
- **Create feedback loops** - Gather lessons learned and measure impact against predetermined metrics
- **Invest in foundational skills** - Begin training programs for teams working with generative AI tools

#### Example Use Cases for Crawl Phase:
- Document summarization and Q&A from internal knowledge bases
- Draft email and report writing assistance
- Code explanation and simple code generation
- Customer support chatbot for common FAQs
- Marketing copywriting for social media posts

#### Expected Outcomes:
- Validated business value from initial use cases
- Internal expertise developed in small teams
- Risk mitigation strategies identified
- Organizational comfort level increases with the technology

---

### 2. **Walk** - Standardization and Scale Phase

**Objective:** Expand proven use cases across the organization while establishing mature processes and governance.

#### Key Characteristics:
- **Scope:** Department-wide or cross-functional rollout
- **Risk Level:** Medium (broader exposure, well-defined processes)
- **Investment:** Increased but justified by proven ROI
- **Governance:** Formalized processes, automated controls where possible
- **Metrics:** Performance, adoption, cost, business impact

#### Activities in the Walk Phase:
- **Standardize tools and platforms** - Select approved generative AI tools and consolidate vendor relationships
- **Develop playbooks and best practices** - Document proven approaches from the Crawl phase
- **Implement formal governance** - Establish policies for data handling, model selection, audit trails, and compliance
- **Scale infrastructure and security** - Ensure systems can handle broader usage while maintaining security and compliance
- **Expand training programs** - Roll out structured training to larger teams
- **Measure and monitor continuously** - Establish KPIs, dashboards, and monitoring to track:
  - Adoption rates
  - Quality of AI-generated outputs
  - Cost per use case
  - User satisfaction
  - Compliance violations
- **Integrate with existing systems** - Connect generative AI tools to enterprise applications and workflows
- **Build center of excellence (CoE)** - Establish a dedicated team to oversee generative AI adoption and support business units
- **Address change management** - Conduct comprehensive change management to help teams adapt workflows

#### Example Use Cases for Walk Phase:
- Enterprise-wide knowledge management and search
- Customer relationship management integration
- Internal process automation and workflow optimization
- Data analysis and insights generation
- Legal and compliance document review
- HR and recruitment assistance

#### Expected Outcomes:
- Generative AI becomes embedded in standard workflows
- Operational efficiencies realized across multiple departments
- Mature security and compliance posture
- Clear ROI metrics established and tracked
- Organizational resistance decreases as value becomes visible

---

### 3. **Run** - Optimization and Innovation Phase

**Objective:** Achieve mature, enterprise-scale operations with continuous optimization and innovation.

#### Key Characteristics:
- **Scope:** Organization-wide or enterprise-level adoption
- **Risk Level:** Well-managed (mature controls, predictable processes)
- **Investment:** Ongoing but optimized for cost and performance
- **Governance:** Automated controls, continuous compliance
- **Metrics:** Advanced analytics, predictive insights, competitive advantage

#### Activities in the Run Phase:
- **Continuous optimization** - Refine models, prompts, and workflows based on real-world performance data
- **Advanced use case development** - Move into complex, strategic use cases:
  - Custom model fine-tuning
  - Multi-agent systems and complex orchestrations
  - Predictive analytics and business intelligence
  - Strategic decision support systems
- **Automate governance and compliance** - Implement automated controls, audit logging, and compliance verification
- **Build proprietary capabilities** - Develop custom models or integrations unique to your organization
- **Drive innovation initiatives** - Establish programs to explore emerging generative AI capabilities and applications
- **Optimize costs** - Balance performance and capability with cost through techniques like:
  - Model selection and routing
  - Prompt optimization
  - Caching and deduplication
  - Reserved capacity
- **Establish partnerships** - Work with vendors, consultants, and industry peers
- **Continuous learning** - Monitor industry trends, update practices, and evolve skills

#### Example Use Cases for Run Phase:
- Personalized customer intelligence systems
- Real-time business insights and anomaly detection
- Autonomous workflow execution and decision-making
- Advanced research and development acceleration
- Strategic scenario modeling and forecasting
- Industry-specific proprietary applications

#### Expected Outcomes:
- Generative AI becomes a core competitive advantage
- Operations are fully automated and optimized
- Continuous innovation becomes part of organizational culture
- Measurable business transformation (revenue growth, cost reduction, time-to-market)
- Industry leadership in generative AI adoption

## Key Principles Across All Phases

### 1. **Iterative and Cyclical**
The framework is not strictly linear. Teams may operate at different maturity levels for different capabilities. Expect to revisit earlier phases as you explore new use cases or technologies.

### 2. **Cross-Functional Collaboration**
Success requires involvement from:
- **Executive leadership** - Vision and resource allocation
- **IT and security teams** - Infrastructure and compliance
- **Business unit leaders** - Use case identification and adoption
- **Data teams** - Data governance and quality
- **HR and change management** - Organizational readiness

### 3. **Risk Management**
Each phase has different risk profiles. Controls should be proportionate to risk:
- **Crawl:** Basic controls, isolated environments
- **Walk:** Standardized controls, integration with enterprise systems
- **Run:** Automated, continuous compliance monitoring

### 4. **Measurement-Driven**
Define success metrics for each phase:
- **Crawl:** Proof of concept, learning outcomes, initial ROI
- **Walk:** Adoption rates, quality metrics, cost per transaction, business impact
- **Run:** Competitive advantage, strategic value, innovation rate

### 5. **Continuous Learning**
Each phase generates organizational knowledge:
- What works and what doesn't
- Which use cases deliver the most value
- How the organization adapts to new technology
- What skills are needed

## Critical Success Factors

### 1. **Executive Sponsorship**
Without clear organizational backing, adoption will stall. Executive leadership must:
- Allocate resources
- Remove organizational barriers
- Model adoption behaviors
- Drive accountability

### 2. **Governance and Guardrails**
Establish clear policies around:
- **Data usage** - What data can be used as input?
- **Intellectual property** - Who owns generated content?
- **Ethical use** - What types of use are prohibited?
- **Compliance** - How do we meet regulatory requirements?
- **Bias and fairness** - How do we prevent discriminatory outcomes?

### 3. **Skilled Teams**
Invest in training and hiring:
- Prompt engineering skills
- AI literacy for business teams
- Data governance expertise
- Security and compliance knowledge

### 4. **Clear Business Cases**
Each use case should have documented:
- Problem being solved
- Expected business impact (quantified)
- Resource requirements
- Success metrics
- Timeline and milestones

### 5. **Organizational Change Management**
Technology adoption is fundamentally about people. Prepare for:
- Workflow changes
- Job role evolution
- Team dynamics shifts
- Potential resistance

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

### **Skipping Phases**
Jumping directly to "Run" without establishing foundations in "Crawl" leads to:
- Inadequate governance
- Poor adoption rates
- Compliance violations
- Unexpected costs

### **Moving Too Slowly**
Spending too long in "Crawl" or "Walk" results in:
- Competitive disadvantage
- Loss of organizational momentum
- Technology aging before full deployment
- Delayed ROI realization

### **Losing Executive Sponsorship**
Without continued leadership support:
- Resource constraints emerge
- Governance breaks down
- Teams revert to old ways
- Adoption stalls

### **Underestimating Governance Needs**
Leaving governance too loose causes:
- Data breaches
- Compliance violations
- Loss of data quality
- Ethical concerns

### **Not Building Skills**
Deploying technology without adequate training results in:
- Underutilization
- Poor quality outputs
- Failed projects
- Team frustration

## Generative AI-Specific Considerations

### **Data and Privacy**
- Generative AI models may retain or share input data
- Establish clear policies about proprietary and personal information
- Implement data classification and access controls
- Consider on-premise or private deployment options for sensitive data

### **Model Selection**
- Different models have different capabilities, costs, and compliance profiles
- Start with proven, vendor-supported models
- Gradually explore specialized or custom models as expertise grows
- Plan for model updates and potential obsolescence

### **Quality and Hallucinations**
- Generative AI can produce convincing but inaccurate outputs
- Implement human review processes proportionate to risk
- Establish feedback mechanisms to improve outputs
- Define acceptable error rates for each use case

### **Ethical and Bias Concerns**
- Models can perpetuate or amplify biases in training data
- Establish ethics review processes
- Monitor for discriminatory outcomes
- Maintain human oversight for high-stakes decisions

### **Cost Management**
- Generative AI services can have unpredictable costs
- Implement usage monitoring and alerts
- Optimize prompts and models for cost efficiency
- Consider reserved capacity for predictable workloads

## Timeline Expectations

While every organization is different, typical timelines are:

- **Crawl Phase:** 3-6 months
  - Select 1-3 pilot use cases
  - Build initial governance framework
  - Develop internal expertise

- **Walk Phase:** 6-12 months
  - Expand to 5-10 use cases
  - Reach 20-40% of eligible workforce
  - Establish standardized processes

- **Run Phase:** 12+ months
  - Continuous optimization and innovation
  - Widespread adoption (70%+)
  - Mature governance and operations

## Applying Crawl, Walk, Run to NHS.net Connect Multi-Tenant Architecture

### Context: Three Environments, Different Azure Tenants

NHS.net Connect operates with three distinct Azure tenants representing different lifecycle stages:
- **Dev Tenant** - Development and experimentation
- **Test Tenant** - Validated, pre-production testing
- **Production Tenant** - Live services serving healthcare organizations

This multi-tenant architecture provides an excellent natural alignment with Crawl, Walk, Run principles, where environment progression mirrors maturity progression.

### Mapping Phases to Environments

#### **Crawl Phase → Dev Tenant**

The Dev tenant is your designated experimentation space for generative AI, perfectly suited to Crawl activities.

**Environment Characteristics:**
- Limited scope: Internal teams and select pilot partners
- Lower compliance bar: Focus on learning rather than full compliance
- Rapid iteration: Quick deployment and testing cycles
- Lower data sensitivity: Use synthetic or non-production data

**Controls & Governance:**
- ✅ **Permissive AI policies** - Allow broad exploration of AI capabilities
- ✅ **Basic audit logging** - Track what's happening but not production-grade
- ✅ **Data classification** - Mark all data as development-only, no PII/PHI
- ✅ **Limited access control** - Restricted to innovation and pilot teams
- ✅ **Manual review processes** - Human approval of outputs before any analysis
- ✅ **No patient/citizen data** - Synthetic datasets only
- ✅ **Cost monitoring** - Track AI usage but allow experimentation

**Development Activities:**
- Explore generative AI capabilities relevant to NHS workflows
- Identify high-value, low-complexity use cases
- Test prompt engineering approaches
- Evaluate different models (GPT-4, Claude, Llama, etc.)
- Develop training materials for staff
- Create initial governance policies
- Build proof-of-concepts with internal teams

**Example Use Cases:**
- Clinical documentation summarization (non-patient data)
- Medical literature Q&A systems
- Administrative workflow automation
- Internal process optimization
- Staff training materials generation
- Research data analysis assistance

**Success Metrics:**
- Number of use cases validated
- Staff familiarity with AI tools
- Governance policies drafted
- Cost benchmarks established
- Risk mitigation strategies identified

**Typical Duration:** 3-6 months

---

#### **Walk Phase → Test Tenant**

The Test tenant serves as your bridge to production, where proven use cases are hardened and governance is formalized.

**Environment Characteristics:**
- Expanded scope: Multiple departments across NHS.net Connect partners
- Medium compliance bar: Production-like governance but with controlled rollout
- Controlled iteration: Changes managed through formal change control
- Production-like data: Realistic datasets, carefully anonymized or synthetic

**Controls & Governance:**
- ✅ **Formal AI use policies** - Documented, approved policies for AI usage
- ✅ **Comprehensive audit logging** - Full audit trails of all AI interactions
- ✅ **Strict data classification** - PII/PHI handling policies enforced
- ✅ **Role-based access control (RBAC)** - Teams have specific, documented permissions
- ✅ **Automated quality checks** - Validation rules for AI outputs
- ✅ **Compliance alignment** - GDPR, Data Protection Act, NHS standards
- ✅ **Documented review processes** - Clear escalation and approval workflows
- ✅ **Cost controls** - Budgets and alerts for AI spending
- ✅ **No live patient data** - Anonymized or synthetic datasets for realistic testing
- ✅ **Security testing** - Penetration testing and vulnerability assessments

**Implementation Activities:**
- Standardize on approved AI models and services
- Implement formal governance and compliance controls
- Deploy monitoring and logging infrastructure
- Establish center of excellence (CoE) for AI oversight
- Conduct staff training at scale
- Test all use cases with production-like scenarios
- Validate compliance with NHS information governance requirements
- Perform security assessments
- Document runbooks and operational procedures

**Example Use Cases (Expanded):**
- NHS email template generation (anonymized data)
- Clinical guidelines Q&A systems
- Appointment scheduling assistance
- Prescription review support (with human approval)
- Patient communication draft generation
- Workforce planning analysis
- Resource optimization recommendations

**Governance Structure:**
- **AI Governance Board** - Approves new use cases and policy changes
- **Security & Compliance Team** - Ensures adherence to regulations
- **CoE Team** - Supports departments, manages best practices
- **Data Stewards** - Oversee data quality and classification
- **Audit Function** - Reviews logs and ensures compliance

**Success Metrics:**
- Use case expansion: 5-10 validated use cases
- Adoption rate: 20-40% of eligible teams
- Compliance: 100% policy adherence
- Quality metrics: Output accuracy and appropriateness
- Cost efficiency: Cost per transaction established
- Risk incidents: Zero material breaches

**Typical Duration:** 6-12 months

### Multi-Tenant Security & Compliance Strategy

#### **Data Governance Across Tenants**

| Aspect | Dev Tenant | Test Tenant | Production Tenant |
|--------|-----------|------------|-------------------|
| **Data Type** | Synthetic only | Anonymized/synthetic | Real (consented) |
| **PII/PHI Present** | None | Limited/anonymized | Full |
| **Data Retention** | 30 days | 90 days | Compliant with policy |
| **Backup/DR** | None required | Standard backups | Full DR setup |
| **Access** | Internal team | Internal + selected partners | All authorized users |
| **Encryption** | In-transit only | In-transit & at-rest | In-transit & at-rest + HSM |

#### **Compliance Maturity Across Phases**

| Control | Dev | Test | Production |
|---------|-----|------|------------|
| **GDPR Compliance** | Design | Enforced | Audited |
| **NHS IG Toolkit** | Aligned | Assessed | Verified |
| **Data Protection Act** | Baseline | Implemented | Monitored |
| **Consent Management** | N/A | Tested | Operational |
| **Audit Logging** | Basic | Detailed | Comprehensive |
| **Incident Response** | Manual | Documented | Automated |
| **Vendor Management** | Informal | Formal contracts | SLA-based |

#### **AI-Specific Controls**

**Across All Tenants:**
- ✅ Model provenance tracking - Know which models run where
- ✅ Prompt auditing - Log all prompts to detect inappropriate use
- ✅ Output validation - Verify AI outputs before use
- ✅ Bias monitoring - Detect and mitigate discriminatory outputs
- ✅ Hallucination detection - Flag confidence levels in outputs
- ✅ Model explainability - Understand why AI made decisions
- ✅ User feedback loops - Continuous quality improvement

**Escalation by Tenant:**
- **Dev:** Manual review by team lead
- **Test:** Automated checks + manual escalation for edge cases
- **Production:** Real-time automated validation + manual review for high-stakes decisions

### Migration Pathways Between Tenants

#### **Promotion Criteria**

**Dev → Test Promotion:**
1. Use case documented with business case
2. Proof-of-concept completed successfully
3. Risk assessment conducted
4. Initial governance policies drafted
5. At least 2 stakeholders trained
6. Cost benchmarks established
7. Data governance requirements defined
8. Compliance assessment completed

**Test → Production Promotion:**
1. Successful testing with realistic scenarios (minimum 4 weeks)
2. Governance policies formally approved
3. Compliance validation passed (internal + external audit)
4. Security assessment passed (penetration testing)
5. Staff training completed (all users trained)
6. Runbooks and escalation procedures documented
7. Monitoring and alerting configured
8. Disaster recovery tested
9. Financial approval and budget allocated
10. Clinical/business stakeholder sign-off

#### **Rollback Plan**

Each tenant should have:
- **Rollback criteria** - When to roll back (accuracy <90%, compliance violation, security incident)
- **Rollback timeline** - How quickly we can deactivate AI service (target: <1 hour)
- **Fallback procedures** - How to continue operations without AI (manual processes documented)
- **Communication plan** - How to notify stakeholders of rollback

## Conclusion

Microsoft's Crawl, Walk, Run framework provides a proven approach to managing the introduction of generative AI into productive enterprise environments. By proceeding deliberately through phases of experimentation, standardization, and optimization, organizations can:

✅ Minimize risk while maximizing learning  
✅ Build organizational capability and skills  
✅ Establish mature governance and compliance  
✅ Deliver measurable business value  
✅ Position for competitive advantage  
✅ Enable continuous innovation  

For NHS.net Connect specifically, the natural alignment between the framework's phases and the multi-tenant architecture provides a clear pathway: use the **Dev tenant for experimentation and learning**, the **Test tenant for standardization and validation**, and the **Production tenant for mature, compliant, optimized operations**. This approach ensures that generative AI adoption is secure, compliant with NHS and UK data governance requirements, and delivers measurable clinical and operational value.

The key is recognizing that technology adoption is a **journey, not a destination**—with each phase and tenant building on the lessons and capabilities of the previous one, while maintaining appropriate controls and governance proportionate to the risk profile of each environment.
